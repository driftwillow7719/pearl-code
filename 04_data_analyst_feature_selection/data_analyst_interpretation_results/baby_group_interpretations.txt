GROUP AND PAIRWISE INTERPRETATION DIGEST
Popularity-trajectory dataset: items segmented by current popularity level (zero / medium / high) crossed with outcome direction (decrease / stable / increase), and further split by how much history each item has (debut = item's release month, no trend point at all; recent_single_point = exactly one historical observation, item_age fixed at 0; recent_multi_point = two or more observations, item_age 1 or 3 months; maturing = item_age above 3 months, unbounded).

A note on sample sizes before the group-by-group detail: the correlation tests reported below for each (group, history-depth bucket) combination are computed against a binary indicator of "did this item end up in this direction" versus the other directions within the same popularity level and the same history-depth bucket. Because of this, the sample size attached to a given correlation is the total pool of items in that popularity level and bucket (all directions combined), not just the count of items that ended up in the specific direction being discussed. Where it materially changes how much weight a figure deserves, the count of items that actually belong to the group itself (the "true" group size) is also given.

================================================================================
HIGH_POPULAR / DECREASE
================================================================================

GROUP DEFINITION
This is the segment of currently high-popularity items whose popularity subsequently decreased (260 items). Their latest observed popularity value is typically 6 (middle 50% of items: 5 to 9; full range: 4 to 32). Item age is typically 11 months (middle 50%: 1 to 24 months), meaning this group skews toward more mature items but with real spread. Across their historical trend, the lowest point reached is typically 0 (middle 50%: 0 to 1; full range up to 19), and the highest point reached is typically 8 (middle 50%: 6 to 12; full range up to 53). Volatility (variability of the trend) is typically 2.13 (middle 50%: 1.54 to 3.18; full range up to 14.5), and the trend rose by a net of 5 units from its start to its end on average. The most recent peak in the trend typically occurred 0 months before the latest observation (middle 50%: 0 to 4), i.e. many items were still near their peak just before turning downward. Momentum (rate of recent change) is typically 0.511 (middle 50%: 0.134 to 1.1; full range: -1.52 to 2.4), but this could only be computed for 228 of the 260 items — the other 32 lacked enough trend history to support it. Separately, and only as a description of what already happened to these labeled items (not a usable feature for a new query item), items in this group lost an average of 2 popularity units between their last observed value and their eventual outcome (middle 50%: -3 to -1; full range: -23 to -1). None of the 260 items were debut items, and 32 of the 260 had only a single real historical point.

DATA RELIABILITY NOTE
The 260-item group breaks into a maturing sub-bucket of 177 items, a recent_multi_point sub-bucket of only 51 items, and a recent_single_point sub-bucket of only 32 items — the latter two are small enough that any typical-value or correlation claim specific to them should be treated as indicative rather than precise. The correlation tests below are run against larger pools (318, 218, and 1,073 items respectively) because they include every direction outcome within the same popularity level and bucket, not just the 260 decrease items; this makes the correlation estimates themselves reasonably stable even where the decrease-specific sub-bucket is small, but narrative typical-value claims tied to the 32- or 51-item sub-buckets specifically should be read with that caution in mind.

KEY FEATURE-OUTCOME RELATIONSHIPS
Where an item has two or more historical points, the strongest and most usable signals are the trend-shape features. In the recent_multi_point bucket (pool of 218 items across all outcomes at this bucket), the single largest driver is the latest observed popularity value itself, which favors this decreasing outcome strongly (r=0.668, p=1.7e-29, n=218) — in plain terms, high-popularity items that are already sitting at a high current value are the ones most likely to be heading down. Close behind, a high trend maximum also favors decrease (r=0.664, p=4.2e-29, n=218), the total change over the trend favors decrease when it has been rising overall (r=0.605, p=4.1e-23, n=218), and volatility strongly favors decrease as well (r=0.600, p=1.1e-22, n=218) — together these say that items with a high peak, a volatile history, and an overall upward run are the ones most likely about to reverse. A high trend minimum also favors decrease moderately (r=0.487, p=2.1e-14, n=218), strong recent upward momentum favors decrease (r=0.435, p=1.9e-11, n=218), and having recently passed its peak (a smaller time-since-peak) favors decrease (r=-0.461, p=7.5e-13, n=218). Item age itself carries a real but modest signal in this bucket, with younger items in this narrow 1-to-3-month window somewhat more likely to be the ones falling (r=-0.209, p=0.0020, n=218). Embedding similarity to comparable items shows no meaningful relationship here (r=0.045, p=0.51, n=218).

In the maturing bucket (pool of 1,073 items), the same overall pattern holds but weaker: the latest popularity value remains the top predictor (r=0.549, p=1.8e-85, n=1,073), followed by the trend's overall change (r=0.517, p=2.2e-74), the trend maximum (r=0.385, p=3.1e-39), and volatility (r=0.380, p=4.1e-38). Momentum is next (r=0.325, p=8.6e-28), and a smaller time-since-peak still favors decrease (r=-0.257, p=1.2e-17). The trend minimum and item age are both weak but real here (r=0.120, p=8.6e-05 and r=0.120, p=8.3e-05 respectively, both n=1,073) — essentially negligible on their own but not spurious. Embedding similarity again shows nothing (r=-0.040, p=0.19).

For items with only a single historical point (pool of 318), most trend-shape features cannot be computed at all — there is no way to derive a minimum, maximum, volatility, overall change, time-since-peak, or momentum from one observation, so those cells are undefined rather than weak. What remains testable still carries real signal: the single latest popularity value is a strong predictor even here (r=0.543, p=8.8e-26, n=318), meaning that even with minimal history, a high current popularity value already points toward an eventual decrease for high-popularity items. Embedding similarity is not informative (r=0.024, p=0.67, n=318), and item age has no variance to test in this bucket by definition, since every item with exactly one observation has item_age fixed at 0.

ITEM_AGE-LINKED PATTERNS
Scanning every feature's relationship with item age specifically for this group (pooled across buckets, using a threshold of p<0.01 and a correlation magnitude of at least 0.10 to call a relationship real): the trend minimum shows the strongest age relationship by far, declining as items get older (r=-0.690, p=1.2e-33, n=228) — this is also one of this group's genuinely useful outcome predictors, so its predictive value should be understood as partly reflecting how far along in its life the item is. Time-since-peak rises with item age, unsurprisingly (r=0.526, p=1.2e-17, n=228), and is likewise a real outcome predictor here, so the two effects overlap. Momentum tends to decline with age (r=-0.316, p=1.2e-06, n=228) and volatility also declines with age (r=-0.301, p=3.6e-06, n=228); both of these are meaningful outcome predictors too, so part of their signal is standing in for item age. The latest popularity value shows a real but comparatively modest decline with age (r=-0.217, p=4.4e-04, n=260). By contrast, the trend maximum — despite being one of the strongest outcome predictors in this group — shows no reliable relationship with item age (r=-0.141, p=0.033, which misses the stricter significance bar), and the trend's overall change likewise shows no real age dependence (r=-0.070, p=0.29). Embedding similarity is unrelated to age as well (r=-0.096, p=0.12).

ONE-LINE TAKEAWAY
For this group, the latest popularity value and the trend's overall change/volatility/maximum are the most decision-relevant features, and a feature selector should keep in mind that several of the strongest ones (trend minimum, time-since-peak, momentum, volatility) are meaningfully entangled with how old the item is rather than being fully independent signals.

HIGH_POPULAR / INCREASE
================================================================================

GROUP DEFINITION
This is the segment of currently high-popularity items whose popularity subsequently increased further. Excluding debut items, there are 1,208 such items (a further 239 debut items also belong to this outcome and are discussed separately below, since debut items have no computable trend at all). For the 1,208 non-debut items, the latest observed popularity value is typically 1 (middle 50%: 1 to 2; full range: 0 to 23) — notably lower than the decreasing group's typical value of 6, consistent with these being items that still had room to climb. Item age is typically 7 months (middle 50%: 1 to 17). Across the trend, the low point is typically 0 (middle 50%: 0 to 0; full range up to 7) and the high point is typically 3 (middle 50%: 2 to 5; full range up to 31). Volatility is typically 1.0 (middle 50%: 0.687 to 1.5; full range up to 8.08), with essentially no net change from start to end of the trend on average. The peak in the historical trend typically occurred 4 months before the latest observation (middle 50%: 2 to 8) — later than in the decrease group, meaning these items were further past any earlier peak before climbing again. Momentum is typically 0 (middle 50%: -0.606 to 0.405; full range: -1.95 to 1.61), computed for 938 of the 1,208 items (270 lacked sufficient history). As a purely historical description of already-labeled items (not a usable per-item feature), these items gained an average of 3 popularity units from their last observed value to their eventual outcome (middle 50%: +2 to +4; full range: +1 to +30).

DATA RELIABILITY NOTE
The non-debut portion breaks into a maturing sub-bucket of 792 items (a reasonably solid sample), a recent_single_point sub-bucket of 270 items, and a smaller recent_multi_point sub-bucket of 146 items. The 239 debut items are a separate, larger sub-population handled in the structural note below. Correlation tests are run against pools of 1,073 (maturing), 318 (recent_single_point), and 218 (recent_multi_point) items spanning all three outcome directions at that bucket, so the correlation estimates themselves rest on decent sample sizes even where the increase-specific count is smaller.

KEY FEATURE-OUTCOME RELATIONSHIPS
In the recent_multi_point bucket (pool of 218), the strongest signal is the latest popularity value, which favors increase when it is low (r=-0.711, p=7.5e-35, n=218) — i.e. items currently sitting at a low popularity value are the ones most likely to climb. A high trend maximum favors increase when the item is heading up rather than already having peaked (r=-0.683, p=2.9e-31), and a large overall rise across the trend favors this outcome (r=-0.638, p=2.3e-26). High volatility also favors increase here (r=-0.599, p=1.3e-22), a high trend minimum favors increase (r=-0.501, p=3.2e-15), and a longer time since the last peak favors increase (r=0.498, p=4.6e-15) — together these describe items that dipped a while ago and are volatile enough to be turning back up. Strong recent momentum favors increase as well (r=-0.451, p=2.7e-12). Item age carries a real, if modest, signal in this bucket: older items (within the 1-to-3-month range) are somewhat more likely to be increasing (r=0.188, p=0.0052, n=218). Embedding similarity is not informative (r=-0.084, p=0.22).

In the maturing bucket (pool of 1,073), the latest popularity value remains dominant (r=-0.628, p=1.1e-118), followed closely by the trend's overall change (r=-0.610, p=1.6e-110). Momentum is next (r=-0.381, p=2.3e-38), essentially tied with volatility (r=-0.381, p=1.7e-38), then the trend maximum (r=-0.394, p=3.1e-41, actually slightly ahead of momentum and volatility in magnitude), and a longer time-since-peak (r=0.288, p=6.3e-22). Item age is a real, modest predictor here too, with older items less likely to be increasing (r=-0.150, p=8.6e-07, n=1,073), and the trend minimum is a weak but real signal (r=-0.105, p=5.4e-04). Embedding similarity remains uninformative (r=0.016, p=0.60).

For the recent_single_point bucket (pool of 318, one real observation per item), only the latest popularity value, embedding similarity, and item age can even be evaluated, since every trend-shape feature that needs two or more points is undefined here by construction. The latest popularity value is a strong predictor even with this minimal history: a low current value favors increase (r=-0.531, p=1.6e-24, n=318). Embedding similarity is not useful (r=-0.024, p=0.67), and item age has no variance to test since it is fixed at 0 for every item in this bucket.

ITEM_AGE-LINKED PATTERNS
Time-since-peak has by far the strongest relationship with item age in this group, growing steadily as items age (r=0.529, p=8.2e-69, n=938) — since time-since-peak is also one of this group's real outcome predictors in both trend-carrying buckets, a meaningful part of its predictive power should be understood as tracking item age itself rather than an independent signal. The trend minimum declines with age (r=-0.470, p=1.3e-52, n=938), the trend maximum rises with age (r=0.336, p=3.9e-26), and volatility rises with age (r=0.222, p=6.3e-12) — all three are also real outcome predictors in this group, so each carries some age-driven overlap. The trend's overall change shows a real, positive relationship with age (r=0.167, p=2.8e-07, n=938). By contrast, the latest popularity value — the single strongest outcome predictor in this group — shows only a statistically detectable but practically negligible relationship with age (r=-0.082, p=0.0045, n=1,208): the significance here is a product of the large sample rather than a meaningful effect, so this group's dominant predictor should be treated as largely independent of item age. Momentum and embedding similarity show no real age relationship (r=-0.078, p=0.017 and r=-0.045, p=0.12 respectively).

ONE-LINE TAKEAWAY
The latest popularity value is this group's most decision-relevant and most age-independent feature, while time-since-peak, trend minimum, trend maximum, and volatility are also strong predictors but should be read knowing they partly track how mature the item is.

HIGH_POPULAR / STABLE
================================================================================

GROUP DEFINITION
This is the segment of currently high-popularity items whose popularity stayed roughly stable (141 items). Their latest popularity value is typically 4 (middle 50%: 3 to 5; full range: 3 to 16). Item age is typically 13 months (middle 50%: 3 to 24), the oldest typical age among the three high_popular outcomes. Across the trend, the low point is typically 0 (middle 50%: 0 to 1; full range up to 5) and the high point is typically 5 (middle 50%: 4 to 7; full range up to 24). Volatility is typically 1.52 (middle 50%: 1.09 to 2.03; full range up to 8.6), with a modest net rise of 2 units across the trend. The peak typically occurred 1 month before the latest observation (middle 50%: 0 to 5). Momentum is typically 0.288 (middle 50%: -0.182 to 0.916; full range: -0.916 to 2.08), computed for 125 of the 141 items (16 lacked sufficient history). As a purely historical description, these items showed essentially zero net change from their last observed value to their eventual outcome (middle 50%: 0 to 0; full range: 0 to 0) — consistent with "stable" by definition. None were debut items, and 16 of 141 had only a single historical point.

DATA RELIABILITY NOTE
This is the smallest of the nine groups. The maturing sub-bucket has 104 items, which is workable, but the recent_multi_point sub-bucket has only 21 items and the recent_single_point sub-bucket only 16 — any figure specific to those two sub-buckets is essentially anecdotal and should be treated as suggestive at best. The correlation pools used for testing (318, 218, and 1,073 across the three buckets) are shared with the other two high_popular outcomes and are themselves reasonably sized, but because "stable" is the rarest outcome at the high-popularity level, several features fail to reach significance here simply for lack of stable-outcome cases to distinguish, not necessarily because no real relationship exists.

KEY FEATURE-OUTCOME RELATIONSHIPS
This group shows much weaker separability than the decrease and increase groups, which is itself informative: high-popularity items are harder to identify as "staying put" than as clearly rising or falling. In the maturing bucket (pool of 1,073), the strongest signal, though still modest, is a high trend maximum, which favors stability (r=0.245, p=4.7e-16, n=1,073) — plausible items that reached a solid peak and held near it. The trend's overall change also modestly favors stability (r=0.258, p=7.7e-18), a longer time-since-peak is a small negative signal (r=-0.105, p=5.5e-04), and a slightly older item age modestly favors stability (r=0.072, p=0.018). Volatility (r=0.091, p=0.0030) and momentum (r=0.158, p=1.8e-07) are weak but real. Embedding similarity and the trend minimum are not informative (r=0.026, p=0.39 and r=0.007, p=0.83, respectively). In the recent_multi_point bucket (pool of 218, only 21 true stable members), a high trend maximum is the leading signal (r=0.135, p=0.046) though it only barely clears conventional significance and should be treated cautiously given the small stable-outcome count; the latest popularity value (r=0.175, p=0.0095) and time-since-peak (r=-0.133, p=0.050) are similarly marginal. Given how few true stable cases populate this bucket, none of these should be treated as strong evidence on their own — per the group's general sparsity, even a modest, borderline signal here is still the most useful evidence available for this bucket rather than something to discard. In the recent_single_point bucket (pool of 318, only 16 true stable members), none of the tested features (latest popularity value r=0.122, p=0.029; embedding similarity r=0.007, p=0.90) show a relationship strong enough to trust given how few stable cases exist in this narrow slice, though the latest popularity value's weak signal is still the best available evidence for this sparse bucket.

ITEM_AGE-LINKED PATTERNS
The trend minimum shows the strongest relationship with item age in this group, declining sharply as items age (r=-0.652, p=1.8e-16, n=125), and time-since-peak rises steadily with age as expected (r=0.505, p=1.8e-09, n=125) — both are also real, if modest, outcome predictors here, so their signal is partly a proxy for item maturity. The trend's overall change shows a borderline relationship with age that does not clear the stricter bar used here (r=0.221, p=0.013, n=125) and should not be treated as a confirmed pattern. No other feature in this group shows a real item-age relationship: the trend maximum (r=0.138, p=0.12), embedding similarity (r=-0.125, p=0.14), the latest popularity value (r=0.117, p=0.17), momentum (r=-0.089, p=0.32), and volatility (r=-0.052, p=0.56) are all statistically indistinguishable from no relationship.

ONE-LINE TAKEAWAY
Given how weakly this outcome separates from the others, the trend maximum and the trend's overall change are the most useful (if still modest) signals available, and any feature chosen here should be treated with more caution than in the decrease or increase groups because of the small number of true stable cases behind every estimate.

KEY FEATURE-OUTCOME RELATIONSHIPS
In the recent_single_point bucket (pool of 1,276), where trend-shape features cannot be computed at all, the latest popularity value alone is a very strong predictor: a high current value strongly favors this decrease outcome (r=0.832, p≈0, n=1,276). In the recent_multi_point bucket (pool of 1,459), the latest popularity value remains the strongest signal (r=0.738, p=4.3e-251), followed closely by the trend's overall change (r=0.616, p=7.6e-153) and the trend maximum (r=0.567, p=1.1e-124). The trend minimum is a moderate signal (r=0.163, p=1.4e-37). The trend minimum has the strongest relationship with item age in this group by a wide margin, declining as items get older (r=-0.599, p=1.2e-94, n=960); because the trend minimum is also a real, moderate outcome predictor in this group (especially in the recent_multi_point bucket), a meaningful part of its signal should be understood as tracking item maturity. Time-since-peak rises with age as expected (r=0.513, p=1.1e-65, n=960) and is likewise one of this group's real predictors, so the same caveat applies. The trend's overall change shows a real but more modest age relationship (r=0.151, p=2.4e-06, n=960) despite being one of this group's two strongest outcome predictors overall — its predictive power is therefore only lightly confounded with item age, not heavily. No other feature clears a real age relationship in this group: the trend maximum (r=0.062, p=0.054), the latest popularity value (r=0.050, p=0.089), volatility (r=-0.021, p=0.51), and embedding similarity and momentum (both essentially zero, p>0.5) all fail to show a dependable pattern — notably, the latest popularity value's dominance as an outcome predictor is essentially independent of item age.

ONE-LINE TAKEAWAY
The latest popularity value and the trend's overall change are this group's most decision-relevant features and are only lightly (or not at all) tied to item age, while the trend minimum and time-since-peak, though also strong predictors, carry a meaningful item-age component that a feature selector should keep in mind.

ITEM_AGE-LINKED PATTERNS
Time-since-peak has an exceptionally strong relationship with item age in this group — the strongest of any feature examined across all nine groups — rising sharply as items get older (r=0.786, p≈0, n=4,845). Because time-since-peak is also a real, moderate outcome predictor here in both trend-carrying buckets, this is a prominent caveat: a substantial share of its predictive value should be attributed to item age rather than to an independent trend signal. The trend maximum also shows a real, moderate rise with age (r=0.283, p=5.0e-90, n=4,845) and is likewise a genuine outcome predictor, so the same caution applies, though less severely. The trend minimum shows a real decline with age (r=-0.138, p=6.4e-22) and is a moderate outcome predictor too. The latest popularity value — the single dominant outcome predictor in this group — shows a real but comparatively modest decline with age (r=-0.181, p=2.7e-38, n=5,011); its predictive dominance is therefore only partly, not primarily, a reflection of item age. Momentum shows a real but weak relationship (r=0.104, p=4.2e-13, n=4,845), just clearing the threshold used here. By contrast, the trend's overall change and volatility — despite the former being one of this group's two strongest outcome predictors — show relationships with age too small to trust (r=0.061, p=2.4e-05 and r=0.036, p=0.011, respectively): both are statistically detectable mainly because of the very large sample, not because of a meaningful effect, so the overall change's strong predictive power should be read as essentially independent of item age. ================================================================================
DEBUT ITEMS ACROSS GROUPS (STRUCTURAL NOTE)
================================================================================
Debut items — those observed in their release month, with no historical trend point at all — cannot be analyzed with the standard per-group breakdown used above, for two compounding reasons. First, by definition, every trend-derived feature (the trend minimum and maximum, volatility, overall change, time-since-peak, momentum) and item age itself are undefined or constant for every debut item, so there is no within-bucket variation to correlate against outcome for any of these features. Second, and more strikingly, in the data provided every single debut item across the entire dataset reached the increase outcome: 239 debut items belong to the high_popular/increase group and 5,979 debut items belong to the medium_popular/increase group, for a combined pooled total of 6,218 debut items, and there are zero debut items recorded under any decrease or stable outcome, at any popularity level, anywhere in the data. This is reflected directly in the pooled debut correlation test supplied: even embedding similarity — the one feature that is in principle computable for a debut item — could not be tested against the debut outcome contrast, because the outcome itself (increase versus stable) has no variation to correlate against within debut items; every debut item's outcome is the same value.

Practically, this means that for a genuinely new, unlabeled debut item (no popularity history at all), the reachable outcome set observed in this data is "increase" only. A "decrease" or "stable" outcome directly from debut status is not represented anywhere in the training data supplied here — this is an empirical pattern found in every debut case observed, not a proven logical impossibility, since no independent mechanism guaranteeing it was supplied. Zero_popular has no debut items in this data at all (0 recorded), so no equivalent statement can be made for that popularity level; debut status in this dataset is exclusively associated with items that were already assessed as medium- or high-popularity at release.

ONE-LINE TAKEAWAY
For a debut query item, a feature selector should treat "increase" as the only outcome direction this dataset provides any evidence for, and should not expect any trend-derived feature (or item age itself) to carry information, since none of them vary for debut items by construction.

MEDIUM_POPULAR / INCREASE vs. ZERO_POPULAR / STABLE
These two groups are commonly confused because both typically involve items sitting at or near a zero-to-minimal latest popularity value with little dramatic trend movement. In the recent_multi_point bucket (n=4,667 combined across the two groups), the clearest separator is the latest popularity value itself, which favors medium_popular/increase when it is higher (r=-0.248, p<0.0001) — counterintuitively, medium_popular/increase items tend to already show a slightly higher latest value than zero_popular/stable items despite both being low in absolute terms. The trend minimum also favors medium_popular/increase (r=-0.185, p<0.0001), as does the trend maximum, more weakly (r=-0.118, p<0.0001); no other feature clears a reliable threshold in this bucket. In the maturing bucket (n=16,500), the latest popularity value is again the strongest separator, and again favors medium_popular/increase (r=-0.292, p<0.0001), followed by volatility (r=-0.163, p<0.0001, favoring medium_popular/increase) and the trend maximum (r=-0.139, p<0.0001, favoring medium_popular/increase); time-since-peak favors zero_popular/stable, but only weakly and just short of a reliable effect size (r=0.121, p<0.0001). No pairwise data was supplied for the debut or recent_single_point buckets, since zero_popular/stable has no members in either.

It is worth flagging explicitly that time-since-peak, despite being an outcome predictor for both of these groups individually in their own right (moderately for medium_popular/increase, and — dramatically, via its strong item-age relationship — for zero_popular/stable), does not reliably distinguish this specific pair from each other: it falls short of a dependable effect size in the maturing bucket and is not even significant in the recent_multi_point bucket. A feature selector choosing between these two specific candidates should rely on the latest popularity value, the trend maximum, and volatility instead.

Takeaway: when choosing between medium_popular/increase and zero_popular/stable specifically, the latest popularity value is the most reliable discriminator in both testable buckets, with a higher value pointing toward medium_popular/increase.

MEDIUM_POPULAR / STABLE vs. ZERO_POPULAR / DECREASE

Takeaway: when choosing between medium_popular/stable and zero_popular/decrease, the trend's overall change and momentum together are the most reliable discriminators in the two trend-carrying buckets, both pointing toward zero_popular/decrease when they run higher, while the recent_single_point bucket offers only a very weak signal from the latest popularity value.

Takeaway: when choosing between high_popular/increase and medium_popular/decrease, the latest popularity value is a consistently strong discriminator across all three testable buckets (always favoring medium_popular/decrease when higher), with momentum and time-since-peak providing additional, bucket-dependent separation, especially in the maturing bucket where they are the strongest signals available.
